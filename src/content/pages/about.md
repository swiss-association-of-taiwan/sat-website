{{lead}}
And there was a special reason why the timing felt right.
{{/lead}}

Switzerland was about to celebrate the 700th anniversary of the Confederation in 1991. In the first message to the new association, founding President Hans-Ruedi Burkhalter wrote:

> "What better occasion than the 700th Anniversary year of our country's Confederation for us to start a Swiss Association here in Taiwan!"
>
> Hans-Ruedi Burkhalter, founding President, 1990

{{originalLogo}}

The idea was simple: bring together the relatively small Swiss community in Taiwan, welcome friends of Switzerland, keep some familiar traditions alive and make life a little easier for people newly arriving far from home.

A few weeks after SAT was founded, the Tages-Anzeiger reported from Taipei on the launch of the new Swiss association. The original clipping survives in SAT's first bulletin and offers a glimpse of a community that was only just getting started.

## Before websites, email and WhatsApp

Staying connected looked rather different in those days.

There was no SAT website, no group chat and no online event calendar. News, event announcements, committee updates and community information were shared through printed bulletins.

SAT's early Bulletin 00 already included an activity calendar, Jass tournament information, committee contacts and a notice board where members could share announcements with one another. From the beginning, members were encouraged to suggest activities and contribute ideas. More than 35 years later, we still want SAT to work that way.

Later in 1991, the association launched the first issue of TELL, the Swiss bulletin that continued that role for the community. The November 1991 issue included committee news, Swiss National Day photos, Jass, sports, charity activities and even a printed membership list, a small snapshot of Swiss life in Taiwan before the internet.

## From 700 to 750

More than three decades later, the world feels much smaller.

We are well into the first century of a new millennium, and Switzerland is already moving gradually towards another milestone: its 750th anniversary in 2041.

Today we can video-call family in Switzerland in seconds, follow Swiss news as it happens, navigate Taiwan with our phones and stay connected with friends on the other side of the world throughout the day.

Sometimes it is easy to forget that Switzerland is still more than 9,000 kilometres away.

{{emphasis}}
But some things have not changed very much.
{{/emphasis}}

After a while in Taiwan, it can still be surprisingly nice to hear a familiar language, sit around a table with some raclette, enjoy food from home and spend an evening with people who share some of the same experiences.

And newcomers still appreciate meeting someone who already knows their way around.

That is what SAT is still here for.

## Who You Will Meet

SAT is not only for Swiss people who have made Taiwan their long-term home.

Maybe you have just arrived for work. Maybe you are a Swiss exchange student spending a semester in Taiwan and, after a few weeks, starting to miss the food from home.

Perhaps you would like to bring some of the Taiwanese friends you have made along and give them a glimpse of Swiss food, traditions and community life.

You might also be Taiwanese and have lived, worked or studied in Switzerland. Maybe Switzerland became an important part of your life and you would simply enjoy staying connected with it after returning to Taiwan.

Or perhaps you have never lived in Switzerland at all, but have Swiss friends, family connections or simply an interest in the country.

{{emphasis}}
You are all welcome.
{{/emphasis}}

Our events are open regardless of nationality or membership. Come for an apéro, bring your family or friends, have some Swiss food and meet people.

## What We Do

Throughout the year, SAT organizes a mix of informal gatherings and traditional celebrations.

Some are simple weekday apéros where people can drop in, have something to eat and catch up. Others bring the wider community together for occasions such as Swiss National Day and our Christmas Dinner.

Families are part of the community too, and many of our gatherings are a chance for children, newcomers and long-time residents to meet each other in a relaxed setting.

SAT is not about recreating Switzerland in Taiwan. It is about keeping a connection to it while building friendships and a community here.

## A Community Run by Its Community

SAT is a volunteer-run association.

The events do not organize themselves. Someone orders the cheese, talks to the venue, keeps track of registrations, answers messages, manages the finances and makes sure things are ready when everyone arrives.

All committee members do this in an honorary capacity alongside their regular work and lives.
